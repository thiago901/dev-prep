## Scope

The DevPrep application shell and every study surface inside it: Home (the lab
console), Library, Content detail, Flashcards, English Practice, Mock Interview,
Progress, Profile, Admin builder. Visitor mode: **Operate**.

## Audience and task

Mid-to-senior developers rehearsing interview answers out loud, in English,
usually alone and late. The task is one take at a time: read the prompt, speak,
hear yourself, then compare against a model answer. Constraints: microphone
permission is a normal failure state; some sessions are silent by necessity;
mobile is a first-class recording device; nothing may sit between the user and
their answer.

## Direction contract

**THESIS.** The two-channel language lab position: your take on channel one, the
model answer on channel two, and channel two is physically inert until channel
one has signal. It refuses the study dashboard this category always ships — no
stat tiles, no progress rings, no streak number, no greeting banner. The
record-before-reveal rule is not a setting, it is the hardware.

**OWN-WORLD.** A warm acoustically-dead booth ground (near-black brown, never
blue-black), tape-deck chassis panels one step lighter, bone silkscreen legends,
brass VU illumination as the single warm accent, a record red that exists only
while armed, and a sage monitor green for level-OK. Channel two sits cool
blue-grey and desaturated while locked. Components are channel strips, transport
rows, level meters, drill cards, take sheets and lamp legends. Archivo Expanded
for tracked faceplate legends, Archivo for text, JetBrains Mono for code,
counters and timecode. Color appears at edges, lamps and meters; the reading
field stays achromatic.

**STORY.** The visitor understands within one viewport that this thing wants
them to talk, not read. They believe the model answer is being withheld on
purpose and that hearing their own take is the product. They arm the transport
and speak.

**FIRST VIEWPORT.** A thin faceplate header carries the wordmark left and the
channel controls right (language as a real two-position switch). Beneath it, one
loaded deck dominates: the drill card with the current prompt at reading scale,
the transport row fixed directly under it with the record control as the only lit
element, and channel two visible, struck through and inert. To its right a
monitor bank of skill channel strips with levels and state lamps; underneath,
today's take sheet. No card grid, no tiles.

**FORM.** The Language Lab, candidate 1 of my ordered grounded list, chosen by
the user over the assigned roll through the structured fallback channel after the
decision page closed unanswered. Seed key 1736e25a, mode operate, scope
direction, telemetry kind `pick`. Code-led: no image generation exists in this
harness, so the ambition rides in this contract and the signature interaction
below.

**Signature interaction.** The channel-two unlock: finishing a take releases the
locked channel with a mechanical engage, its strike-through clears, its fader
goes live, and the answer unrolls. Compare cross-fades your take against the
model's structure. Motion is transport-mechanical and short: controls depress,
lamps engage, nothing floats. Reduced motion makes lamps static and transitions
instant.

**FINISH.** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the
finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its
provenance

## Memorable moment

The first time channel two is still struck through and silent while the record
lamp is live: the product visibly refuses to answer until you have.
